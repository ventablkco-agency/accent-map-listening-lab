# The Accent Lab — 20 ElevenLabs recording instructions

## Final standardized scripts

**Sentence 1:** I think the water is rather warm, but the bath feels much better.

**Sentence 2:** Yesterday, I travelled by car to the market and bought a large bottle of water.

Use the exact same text, punctuation and wording for all ten accents. Do not add accent names, introductions, phonetic spellings, or extra words to the spoken text.

Target 6–8 seconds per clip. Generate at a natural conversational pace; do not force timing by extreme speed changes. If a voice reads a sentence in under 6 seconds, use a slightly slower natural speed (around 0.92–0.96) only if it remains convincing. If over 8 seconds, modestly increase speed. Never distort the accent.

## ElevenLabs settings

- First test: Eleven Multilingual v2; compare with Eleven v4 only if available and the voice sounds more authentic.
- Speed: start at 0.94; adjust slightly to achieve 6–8 seconds without unnatural pacing.
- Stability: 65% starting point.
- Similarity: 75–80% starting point, if exposed by the selected model.
- Style exaggeration: 0–15% for natural accent demonstration; avoid commercial or theatrical delivery.
- Speaker boost: on if available and it does not make the voice harsh.
- Output: MP3, 44.1 kHz, 128 kbps.
- Generate each accent's two sentences using the same selected voice and settings.

## Quality control before accepting a clip

1. Confirm the voice sounds recognizably like the named regional variety, not merely a generic English voice.
2. Listen for the features described in the Voice Design prompt; do not assume the prompt guarantees them.
3. Reject caricatured, comic, overly theatrical or obviously synthetic pronunciation.
4. Keep the two sentences by the same speaker for each accent.
5. Label every file as AI-generated in the exhibition.
6. If ElevenLabs cannot provide a credible regional voice, mark that accent as pending rather than mislabeling a generic voice.
